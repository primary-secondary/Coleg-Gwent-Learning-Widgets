Unit 2: Fundamentals of Computer Systems

Input, Output & Multifunction Devices

Read each description, identify the device, and decide whether it is an input, output or multifunction device.

How to use this activity: complete both parts of each question, then select Check answers.

Correct answers will lock in. Incorrect answers will not be revealed, so use Try again and keep working until you get them right.

Remember: input devices send data into a computer system; output devices present information from it; multifunction devices do both.

Part A

Core devices

Start with familiar devices and identify both the device and its role.

1.
A device used to enter letters, numbers and commands by pressing keys.
2.
A pointing device moved by hand to control a pointer and select items on screen.
3.
A screen that displays text, images and video produced by the computer.
4.
A device that records sound and converts it into digital data.
5.
A device that produces a permanent paper copy of digital documents or images.
6.
A device that converts digital audio signals into sound that can be heard.

Part B

Specialist devices

These are still common in computer systems, but some are used for more specific tasks.

7.
A device that captures a paper document or photograph and converts it into a digital image.
8.
A device used to read printed barcodes and send the code into a computer system.
9.
A hand-held controller with sticks and buttons used to control games or simulations.
10.
A flat drawing surface used with a stylus to enter drawings, handwriting or precise movements into a computer.
11.
A device that projects a computer image onto a large screen or wall.
12.
A device worn over or in the ears to produce sound privately for the user.

Part C

Challenge: multifunction devices

These devices can both send data into a system and produce an output for the user.

13.
A display that shows information and also detects where the user touches the screen.
14.
A device that can print documents and also scan or copy paper documents.
15.
A wearable audio device that includes speakers for listening and a microphone for speaking.
16.
A headset that displays a computer-generated 3D environment and also tracks the user's head or hand movements.
17.
A payment terminal that displays information to the user and also accepts touch, chip-and-PIN or contactless input.
18.
A device in a car or kiosk that displays information and also accepts commands through touch controls.
